How It Works

Understanding Speaking & Reading Rates

The WordsToTime calculator uses evidence-based speaking rates from the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association (ASHA). Whether you're preparing a wedding toast, a TED Talk, a school presentation, or a podcast script, accurate timing prevents you from going over — or running short.

How the Calculation Works

Time (seconds) = Word Count ÷ WPM × 60. At the average speaking rate of 130 WPM, every 1,000 words equals approximately 7 minutes 41 seconds. The words to minutes calculator shows these values for any word count. Need to plan the other way? Check how many words you need for a 3-minute speech, 5-minute speech, or 10-minute speech.

For presentations with slides, use the presentation timer which applies a 100 WPM effective rate — accounting for slide transitions, pauses, and audience reaction time. For formal debates, visit the debate time calculator. For public speaking events, the public speaking timer covers ceremonies, keynotes, and toasts.

Reading Speed

The average adult reads silently at 238 WPM with good comprehension, and reads aloud at 183 WPM (ASHA). Use the reading time calculator for accurate reading estimates, or take the free reading speed test to measure your personal rate. Compare with the speaking words per minute guide for full research context.

Pro Tips for Speech Timing

  • Write to your target time: Use the words to minutes calculator to find out how many words you need for a specific duration.
  • Practice out loud: Use our Practice Mode timer to time yourself reading your speech and get your real WPM.
  • Add a 10% buffer: Most speakers slow down when nervous. If your speech times at 10 minutes, write for 9 minutes.
  • Count every word: Use the "Paste Text" tab above for an exact word count that includes filler words and transitions.
  • Measure your speed: Use our free reading speed test to find your personal WPM, then set "Custom" in the speed selector for accurate personal results.
  • Check word count pages: For quick answers, see 1,000 words, 1,500 words, or 2,000 words breakdowns.

For reading-specific estimates, visit the reading time calculator. For information on average speaking rates and the research behind these numbers, see our speaking words per minute guide. Want to time your typing? Try the typing time calculator.
